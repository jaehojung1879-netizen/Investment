## 1. External calibration evidence (not our replicated result)

NY Fed researchers' Nov 16 2026 article compares expected three-month job finding
in SCE with realized rates from CPS. Article prose gives 55.5 vs 57.2% in 2014–19
(expected minus realized −1.7pp), 53.0 vs 58.8% from 2022 onward (−5.8pp).
Age 30 or younger shifts from +6.3 to −3.0pp in the subgroup comparison
(Jan2014–Jun2019 vs Jan2022–Mar2026); over 30 from −5.4 to −6.1pp.
This is a published comparison of different surveys, not linked individuals
or investment returns. The CPS job-loss measure is a lower bound; do not
interpret its difference from expectations as a pure forecast bias. Data
and methods have not been independently reproduced here.
https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/11/can-workers-predict-job-finding-and-job-loss/

## 2. Cross-population evidence: AI and work

- Nov2025 SCE (published Apr14 2026): about 62% of respondents expected AI to
  raise unemployment over the next year, 11.6% expected a decrease; 66% of
  employed AI users reported improved personal productivity.
  https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/04/use-of-gen-ai-in-the-workplace-and-the-value-of-access-to-training/
- Aug2024/25/26 NY/northern NJ firm surveys (published Sep1 2026): AI adoption
  services 25/40/61%, manufacturers 16/26/51%. Among service AI users in
  Aug2026, approximately 4% reported AI-related layoffs, 15% less hiring
  and 13% more hiring during preceding six months.
  https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/09/businesses-are-using-ai-to-transform-work-not-cut-jobs/
- Sep–Nov2025 Small Business Credit Survey (published Oct8 2026, 5248 employer
  firms in AI module): expected 12m employment diffusion index +33pp AI users
  vs +15pp nonusers; revenue +48pp vs +21pp. Not growth rates. Adjusted
  association remains, not a causal effect. Realized outcomes not in study.
  https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/10/ai-adoption-and-employment-expectations-evidence-from-a-survey-of-small-business-owners/
- May14 2026 posting event study: relative weakness of highly exposed
  occupations predates ChatGPT, no clear subsequent break or junior/senior
  divergence. No claim that AI has zero labor effects.
  https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/05/do-job-postings-show-early-labor-market-effects-of-ai/

These are NOT common denominators or matched horizons. No subtracting firm
actions from household beliefs. Cross-strata evidence is compatible with
task augmentation, delayed substitution, or adoption selection. Need actual
productivity/industry hiring, pre-adoption trends and independent causal
variation to separate them. Current evidence weakens a simplistic
"adoption = immediate aggregate job losses" interpretation, not all future
displacement scenarios. No security selection/market prediction.
